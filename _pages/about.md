Thanks for visiting my website!

I am a fifth-year Ph.D. Candidate in Finance at the Darla Moore School of Business, University of South Carolina. My research focuses on banking and financial institutions, artificial intelligence, household finance, and international finance. A central theme of my work is how artificial intelligence changes the production and use of information by financial institutions. In particular, I study how AI transforms banks' information production by converting difficult-to-verify, judgment-dependent soft information into standardized hard-information signals. I construct novel bank-level measures of AI adoption using AI patent filings and AI-related employment. My research examines how this transformation affects credit decisions, relationship lending, and banks' informational advantages. I also study who ultimately benefits from these technological gains: financial institutions, borrowers, or consumers.

I am also passionate about teaching. I teach undergraduate Corporate Finance and enjoy helping students connect financial theory with real-world business and investment decisions. In the classroom, I aim to create an engaging and supportive environment where students feel comfortable asking questions, participating in discussions, and developing financial intuition. My research expertise also provides a foundation for teaching AI in Finance. Through my work on AI adoption in financial institutions and my use of machine learning and textual analysis in empirical research, I aim to bring both the economic and empirical sides of AI into the classroom.
